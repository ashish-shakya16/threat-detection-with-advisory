# Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing is an attempt to trick someone into revealing passwords, payment details or other sensitive information, or into running malware, by pretending to be a trusted person or organisation. It can arrive by email, SMS (smishing), phone call (vishing), social media or fake websites.

## Common signs
- Urgent or threatening language ("your account will be closed today").
- Sender address or link domain that does not match the real organisation, or uses look-alike spelling.
- Requests for passwords, one-time codes or payment details. Legitimate services do not ask for these by email.
- Unexpected attachments, especially .zip, .exe, .js, .iso, or Office files asking to enable macros.
- Generic greetings and unusual grammar, although modern phishing can be well written.

## What to do
- Do not click links or open attachments in suspicious messages. Go to the website directly by typing its address.
- Verify unusual requests by contacting the sender through a known, separate channel.
- Report the message to your IT or security team and delete it.
- If you entered a password on a suspicious site, change it immediately from a trusted device and enable multi-factor authentication.
- If you opened an attachment, disconnect from the network and run a full antivirus scan.

## Prevention
- Use multi-factor authentication on all important accounts.
- Use a password manager; it will not autofill passwords on fake domains.
- Keep email filtering and browser protection enabled.
